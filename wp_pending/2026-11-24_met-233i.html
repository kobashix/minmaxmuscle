MET-233i (PF-08653945): Pfizer's Once-Monthly Amylin Peptide Now in Phase 2b — Research Dossier

Research Dossier — Discovery Date: November 24, 2026

Pfizer is quietly advancing one of the most strategically important obesity peptides in development: MET-233i, a once-monthly amylin analogue acquired from Metsera in late 2025. While GLP-1 headlines dominate the obesity space, MET-233i targets a completely different satiety pathway — and it only needs to be injected once a month. Its Phase 2b SOLIS-1 trial has enrolled 940 patients and is testing both MET-233i alone and in combination with Pfizer's monthly GLP-1 agonist berobenatide. If successful, it could become the first fully monthly obesity combination.

What Is MET-233i?

MET-233i (now coded PF-08653945 on Pfizer's official pipeline) is a long-acting synthetic amylin analogue that acts as a dual agonist at two receptors:

  • Amylin receptor — the physiological target of amylin, a pancreatic hormone co-secreted with insulin that regulates postprandial food intake and glucagon suppression
  • Calcitonin receptor — a closely related GPCR that mediates calcium homeostasis and bone metabolism, with an emerging role in metabolic regulation

Developed by Metsera, the compound was acquired by Pfizer in November 2025 — in a competitive process that reportedly outbid Novo Nordisk — and now carries Pfizer's compound code PF-08653945. It is listed as a New Molecular Entity (NME) in Pfizer's Q1 2026 pipeline update at Phase 2.

What sets MET-233i apart from other amylin programs is its pharmacokinetics: a human half-life of approximately 19 days, confirmed in Phase 1, enabling a once-monthly subcutaneous injection schedule. This compares to:

  • Cagrilintide: once-weekly amylin analog (~7-day half-life)
  • Petrelintide: targeting once-monthly (Phase 3 plans in progress)
  • Eloralintide: once-weekly (Phase 2)
  • MET-233i: confirmed once-monthly (~19-day half-life)

Mechanism of Action

Amylin is a 37-amino acid peptide hormone co-secreted by pancreatic beta cells alongside insulin in response to meals. Its physiological roles are distinct from GLP-1:

  • Appetite suppression via brainstem — amylin acts on the area postrema and nucleus tractus solitarius, anatomically separate from the hypothalamic GLP-1 circuits
  • Postprandial glucagon suppression — amylin reduces the glucagon spike after meals, limiting hepatic glucose output
  • Gastric emptying delay — slows gastric emptying to blunt post-meal glucose excursions
  • Central satiety signaling — reduces meal size and caloric intake through central nervous system pathways

Because amylin and GLP-1 target different neurological circuits for appetite regulation, they exhibit additive effects when combined — the rationale behind Novo Nordisk's CagriSema (cagrilintide + semaglutide) and Pfizer's own MET-233i + berobenatide SOLIS-1 combination arm.

Phase 2b SOLIS-1 Trial (NCT07575932)

The pivotal Phase 2b study enrolled approximately 940 participants in a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design with three arms:

  1. MET-233i monotherapy — testing the amylin analog alone vs. placebo
  2. MET-233i + berobenatide — the most anticipated arm: monthly amylin + monthly GLP-1 combination
  3. Placebo

Primary endpoint: percent change in body weight from baseline to Week 52.
Estimated primary completion: August 2027.
No interim or topline data have been announced as of November 2026.

Dosing in Research

In Phase 1, MET-233i was administered as a subcutaneous injection with dose levels explored in ascending cohorts. The 1.2 mg dose showed the clearest efficacy signal (8.4% weight loss at Day 36). The Phase 2b SOLIS-1 trial doses have not been publicly disclosed. The target dosing interval is once monthly based on the confirmed ~19-day half-life — meaning one injection every 30 days vs. the weekly injections required by semaglutide, tirzepatide, and all current approved GLP-1 class agents.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does MET-233i differ from semaglutide and tirzepatide as a weight-loss peptide?

MET-233i (PF-08653945) works through an entirely different receptor pathway than semaglutide and tirzepatide. Semaglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist; tirzepatide adds GIP receptor agonism. Both act primarily via hypothalamic neurons that express GLP-1 receptors. MET-233i is an amylin/calcitonin receptor agonist — it suppresses appetite and food intake via the area postrema and nucleus tractus solitarius in the brainstem, which largely lacks GLP-1 receptors. Because the pathways are distinct, amylin agonists and GLP-1 agonists show additive effects in combination studies. Pfizer's SOLIS-1 trial is specifically testing MET-233i plus berobenatide (a monthly GLP-1 agonist) for this additive benefit. The other key difference is dosing interval: MET-233i has a ~19-day half-life enabling once-monthly injections vs. weekly injections for semaglutide and tirzepatide.

What did MET-233i Phase 1 results actually show?

Metsera reported topline Phase 1 data in 2025. The study enrolled 80 adults with overweight or obesity (BMI ≥ 27 kg/m²) without type 2 diabetes. At the 1.2 mg dose, placebo-subtracted weight loss reached 8.4% at Day 36 — a pharmacokinetic window that represents early response before steady-state monthly dosing is achieved. Dose-dependent weight reduction was observed across all cohorts. The ~19-day human half-life confirmed that once-monthly dosing is feasible. Tolerability was acceptable; GI side effects (nausea, decreased appetite) were the most common. No serious adverse events were reported. These results led directly to Pfizer's decision to advance into the Phase 2b SOLIS-1 trial.

Why did Pfizer acquire Metsera and what does that mean for MET-233i development?

Pfizer acquired Metsera in November 2025 for an undisclosed sum, outbidding Novo Nordisk in a competitive process. The strategic rationale: Pfizer needed a credible once-monthly obesity portfolio to compete with weekly-dosing leaders semaglutide (Novo) and tirzepatide (Lilly), and Metsera offered two key assets — MET-233i (monthly amylin) and MET-097i (monthly GLP-1 agonist, now berobenatide). Together, these two Metsera assets give Pfizer a potential fully monthly amylin + GLP-1 combination. For MET-233i specifically, Pfizer's backing means substantially greater Phase 2b/3 trial resources and a clear regulatory and commercial path.

Research Limitations

  • No Phase 2 efficacy data yet: SOLIS-1 is ongoing; maximum weight loss at steady-state monthly dosing is unknown
  • Phase 1 data are early: 8.4% weight loss at Day 36 is a PK snapshot, not a 52-week outcome
  • Amylin monotherapy ceiling: Prior amylin research (pramlintide, 3× daily injection) showed modest weight loss (~3–5%) as monotherapy; MET-233i's longer half-life may change dynamics but the class has historically underperformed GLP-1 mono-agonists
  • Combination arm is the key signal: SOLIS-1's most important readout is the MET-233i + berobenatide arm — but this requires waiting for August 2027 primary completion
  • Phase 1 source reliability: Phase 1 data are from a secondary/summary source; full publication pending
