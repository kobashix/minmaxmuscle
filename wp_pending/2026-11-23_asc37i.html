ASC37i: The Once-Quarterly Triple Agonist Peptide That Just Entered Human Trials

Research Dossier — Discovery Date: November 23, 2026

On November 23, 2026, Ascletis Pharma announced that the U.S. FDA has cleared an Investigational New Drug (IND) application for ASC37i, and that the company has initiated a Phase 1 first-in-human clinical study in adults with obesity. If the preclinical data translate to humans, ASC37i could become the first obesity peptide dosed once quarterly — a 13-week injection interval that would be a step-change from the weekly injections required by all current GLP-1-class drugs.

What Is ASC37i?

ASC37i (also called ASC37) is a triple peptide agonist targeting three receptors simultaneously:

  • GLP-1R (Glucagon-Like Peptide-1 Receptor) — the same receptor targeted by semaglutide and liraglutide
  • GIPR (Glucose-Dependent Insulinotropic Polypeptide Receptor) — the second target in tirzepatide's dual-agonist approach
  • GCGR (Glucagon Receptor) — the third target shared with Lilly's retatrutide, the most potent obesity triple agonist currently in Phase 3

Developed by Ascletis Pharma (NASDAQ: ASCL) using AI-assisted structure-based drug discovery, ASC37i's distinguishing feature isn't just the triple target profile — it's the delivery technology. ASC37i uses Ascletis' proprietary ULAP (Ultra-Long-Acting Peptide) platform and SALD (Self-Assembling Lipid Depot) formulation to achieve an unprecedented dosing interval of once monthly to once quarterly from a single subcutaneous injection of 1 mL or less.

Mechanism of Action

Triple agonism engages three complementary pathways:

  1. GLP-1R agonism: Suppresses appetite via hypothalamic and brainstem satiety circuits. Slows gastric emptying. Stimulates glucose-dependent insulin secretion. Reduces postprandial glucagon.
  2. GIPR agonism: Improves insulin sensitivity, modulates adipogenesis, potentiates the insulin-stimulating effects of GLP-1 synergistically. The GIP component is what elevates tirzepatide above pure GLP-1 mono-agonists in efficacy.
  3. GCGR agonism: Drives hepatic glucose output, thermogenesis, and fatty acid oxidation — adding a metabolic "burn" component that is absent from GLP-1/GIP dual agonists. GCGR agonism also drives liver fat reduction, making triple agonists mechanistically active against MASLD (metabolic-associated steatotic liver disease) and MASH.

The GCGR addition is what separates the triple agonist class from tirzepatide. Lilly's Phase 3 retatrutide data show 22–28% weight loss at 72–80 weeks, which tracks ahead of tirzepatide in the same programs — suggesting the GCGR component adds meaningfully to weight loss beyond dual agonism.

The SALD Delivery System

After subcutaneous injection, the SALD formulation spontaneously forms an in situ lipid depot at the injection site. ASC37i is slowly and continuously released from this depot over weeks to months. The release rate is programmable by adjusting lipid composition — Ascletis targets a half-life approximately 7 times longer than retatrutide's (which is ~6 days), projecting to ~42 days and enabling monthly or quarterly dosing intervals.

This compares to:

  • Semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy): weekly injection, ~7-day half-life
  • Tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound): weekly injection, ~5-day half-life
  • Retatrutide: weekly injection, ~6-day half-life
  • Cagrilintide (amylin analog): weekly injection, ~7-day half-life
  • Petrelintide (amylin analog): monthly injection (Phase 2 target)
  • ASC37i: projected monthly to quarterly injection (NHP data)

Dosing in Research

No human dosing has been established. The Phase 1 study is a dose-escalation design to determine the maximum tolerated dose and optimal dosing interval. The projected once-monthly to once-quarterly dosing interval is based on NHP PK modeling and the SALD release kinetics. Actual human dosing will be determined by Phase 1 and Phase 2 results.

In DIO mouse studies, doses of 1 nmol/kg (single or repeat subcutaneous doses) were used in head-to-head comparisons. Human equivalent doses cannot be calculated from mouse mg/kg dosing without Phase 1 PK data.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is ASC37i different from retatrutide if both are GLP-1/GIP/glucagon triple agonists?

Both ASC37i and retatrutide (Lilly, Phase 3) target the same three receptors, but they differ in two critical dimensions. First, dosing interval: retatrutide requires weekly injection; ASC37i is designed for once-monthly to once-quarterly injection via its SALD depot technology. In non-human primate studies, ASC37i's half-life was approximately 7-fold longer than retatrutide's. Second, development stage: retatrutide is in Phase 3 trials with a BLA filing planned for Q1 2027, while ASC37i has just entered Phase 1 FIH — no human data are available yet. If the extended dosing interval is confirmed in humans with comparable tolerability, ASC37i would represent a meaningfully different patient experience.

What is the SALD (Self-Assembling Lipid Depot) technology?

SALD is a subcutaneous drug delivery platform that forms an in situ depot at the injection site after administration. ASC37i is slowly and continuously released from this depot over weeks, extending its effective half-life far beyond what the peptide's intrinsic chemistry allows. The release rate is programmable by adjusting the lipid composition. The injection volume is designed to be 1 mL or less — compatible with prefilled pen self-administration. As of November 2026, SALD's performance in humans is unproven; Phase 1 will establish whether the projected monthly/quarterly interval is achievable in practice.

Why does ASC37i follow the BLA pathway instead of the NDA pathway?

ASC37i is classified as a biologic (large peptide macromolecule), which requires a Biologics License Application rather than a New Drug Application. The BLA pathway provides two strategic advantages: (1) 12 years of reference product exclusivity from approval, vs. 5 years for small-molecule drugs; and (2) compounding protection — FDA-regulated compounding pharmacies cannot compound approved biologics, unlike the small-molecule GLP-1 agonists that were widely compounded during 2024–2026 shortage periods. This positions ASC37i to avoid the revenue dilution that affected tirzepatide and semaglutide from compounded versions. Retatrutide (Lilly) is on the same BLA pathway for the same reasons.

Research Limitations

  • No human data: Phase 1 FIH just initiated November 23, 2026. All efficacy, safety, and PK claims are preclinical.
  • Mouse-to-human translation risk: GLP-1-class efficacy in DIO mice has historically overpredicted human weight loss. The 88% superiority to tirzepatide in an 11-day mouse study should not be extrapolated to humans.
  • SALD unproven in humans: The projected once-quarterly dosing interval is based on NHP modeling. First-in-human PK will determine actual half-life and tolerable dosing interval.
  • Tolerability unknowns: GCGR agonism adds glucagon-like effects (nausea, potential hypoglycemia risk in some populations) that require careful Phase 1 safety monitoring. Tolerability of ultra-long-acting depot formulations (which cannot be "paused" once injected) is a specific clinical concern.
  • Company-reported preclinical data: ObesityWeek poster data and NHP PK have not been published in a peer-reviewed journal as of November 2026.
  • Long pipeline ahead: Phase 1 → Phase 2 → Phase 3 for a novel biologic with novel delivery technology typically requires 7–10+ years from first-in-human to approval.
