LSALT Peptide (Metablok): The First-in-Class DPEP1 Inhibitor for Kidney Injury — Research Dossier

Research Dossier — Discovery Date: November 24, 2026

On November 24, 2026, Arch Biopartners announced that Duke University School of Medicine became the first US clinical site to join the Phase II trial of LSALT peptide — a novel anti-inflammatory peptide targeting dipeptidase-1 (DPEP1) to protect kidneys from surgery-related injury. While most peptide research focuses on metabolic health and weight loss, LSALT represents a different frontier: using peptide science to protect organs from the inflammatory damage that follows surgery, ischemia, and critical illness.

What Is LSALT Peptide?

LSALT peptide (trade name Metablok) is a first-in-class synthetic peptide developed by Arch Biopartners (TSXV: ARCH.V, OTCQB: ACHFF.US), a Toronto-based clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company. LSALT targets dipeptidase-1 (DPEP1), a cell-surface enzyme expressed on the microvasculature endothelium of the kidneys and other organs.

Until landmark research published in Cell in 2019, DPEP1 was known primarily as a metabolic enzyme. That research revealed a second, clinically critical role: DPEP1 functions as an adhesion receptor for neutrophils and monocytes — the inflammatory white blood cells that cause acute kidney injury during ischemia-reperfusion. By blocking DPEP1, LSALT prevents these cells from binding to the kidney vasculature and triggering the inflammatory cascade that kills kidney tissue.

Mechanism of Action: The DPEP1 Pathway

Acute kidney injury (AKI) is defined as a sudden reduction in kidney function. In surgical and critically ill patients, the most common cause is ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI): when blood flow to the kidney is temporarily reduced (ischemia) and then restored (reperfusion), a two-phase injury occurs:

  1. Ischemia phase: Oxygen deprivation causes cellular stress and ATP depletion in kidney tubular cells
  2. Reperfusion phase: Blood restoration triggers an inflammatory surge — neutrophils and monocytes flood the microvasculature, adhere to endothelial DPEP1, and release cytokines, reactive oxygen species, and proteases that damage the surrounding tissue

The Cell (2019) paper by Rajan et al. showed that DPEP1 on kidney endothelial cells is the critical mediator of this leukocyte adhesion step. LSALT peptide was designed to occupy the DPEP1 adhesion site, physically blocking neutrophils and monocytes from binding — interrupting the cascade before the inflammatory damage occurs.

Arch frames the kidney-protection strategy around two complementary pathways:

  • LSALT → blocks leukocyte-DPEP1 adhesion (inflammatory IRI pathway)
  • Cilastatin → blocks tubular toxin uptake via DPEP1 (nephrotoxicity pathway — a separate Arch program)

Why Cardiac Surgery Is the Phase II Target

Cardiac surgery-associated AKI (CS-AKI) occurs in 20–30% of on-pump cardiac surgery patients, making it the most common serious complication of heart surgery. The on-pump model creates a highly predictable ischemia-reperfusion event:

  1. Cardiopulmonary bypass temporarily replaces heart and lung function
  2. Kidney perfusion is altered (reduced flow, pulsatility, temperature)
  3. Systemic inflammatory activation from blood-circuit contact further primes leukocytes
  4. Reperfusion on weaning from bypass triggers the DPEP1-mediated adhesion cascade

CS-AKI is associated with:

  • Significantly higher 30-day mortality
  • Prolonged ICU and hospital stays
  • Increased progression to chronic kidney disease
  • Higher lifetime cardiovascular risk

There is no currently approved drug to prevent CS-AKI. If the Phase II trial succeeds, LSALT would address a completely unmet medical need affecting 1–2 million patients annually in North America.

Dosing in Research

LSALT is administered intravenously in a perioperative setting (around the time of surgery), not as a subcutaneous self-injection. The delivery model is a single infusion administered by clinical staff before or during cardiac surgery — not a lifestyle injection like GLP-1 drugs. Specific dose levels for the Phase II trial are not publicly disclosed; Phase I established the safe dose range for the Phase II program.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is DPEP1 and why does blocking it protect the kidneys?

DPEP1 (dipeptidase-1) is a cell-surface enzyme found on kidney microvasculature endothelial cells. Research published in Cell (2019) discovered that DPEP1 acts as an adhesion receptor for neutrophils and monocytes — white blood cells that patrol the bloodstream and trigger inflammation. During ischemia-reperfusion injury, which occurs when blood flow is temporarily cut off and then restored (such as during on-pump cardiac surgery), neutrophils and monocytes flood the kidney and bind to DPEP1 on the vessel wall. This adhesion triggers an inflammatory cascade: oxidative stress, cytokine release, and tubular cell death that together define acute kidney injury. LSALT peptide was designed to block the DPEP1 adhesion site, physically preventing leukocytes from attaching. In mouse IRI models, LSALT reduced leukocyte adhesion, lowered inflammatory markers, and preserved kidney function. DPEP1 is highly expressed in kidney vasculature, giving LSALT inherent tissue specificity.

Who is at risk for cardiac surgery-associated AKI and why does it matter?

CS-AKI occurs in 20–30% of patients undergoing heart surgery on cardiopulmonary bypass (on-pump). It is one of the most common and serious complications of cardiac surgery. The mechanism includes both ischemia-reperfusion injury and hemodilution, inflammation from contact with the bypass circuit, and microemboli. Patients who develop CS-AKI have significantly higher rates of 30-day mortality, prolonged ICU and hospital stays, increased risk of progressing to chronic kidney disease, and higher lifetime cardiovascular risk. Despite this, there is currently no FDA-approved drug to prevent CS-AKI — standard of care is supportive. If LSALT's Phase II trial succeeds, it would fill a major gap in perioperative medicine. The unmet need is estimated at 1–2 million at-risk cardiac surgery patients per year in North America.

Is LSALT only for cardiac surgery or could it apply to other types of kidney injury?

The current Phase II trial (NCT05879432) focuses specifically on cardiac surgery-associated AKI because that population provides the most controlled, predictable ischemia-reperfusion event — ideal for a proof-of-concept study. However, the DPEP1 mechanism is not exclusive to cardiac surgery. DPEP1-mediated leukocyte adhesion is a general feature of kidney ischemia-reperfusion injury regardless of cause. Arch previously ran a completed trial of LSALT in COVID-19 patients at risk for ARDS and AKI (NCT04402957). Other potential applications include contrast-induced AKI (from imaging procedures), transplant-associated IRI (a major cause of delayed graft function), and sepsis-associated AKI — all requiring separate clinical trials. The CS-AKI Phase II is the company's pivotal path to initial approval; a positive result would likely trigger expanded indication trials.

Research Limitations

  • Phase II trial ongoing — no efficacy data yet: Primary completion not yet announced; no interim or topline data published
  • Animal-to-human translation uncertainty: Mouse IRI models show promise, but translational success rates for anti-inflammatory compounds in AKI have historically been low
  • Sponsor-associated publications: Key preclinical data come from Arch-associated research; independent replication from non-sponsor groups is limited
  • Niche indication: CS-AKI affects a specific patient population (cardiac surgery patients); broader AKI applications require additional trials
  • IV-only delivery: Intravenous perioperative administration limits self-use; this is a hospital-only intervention
