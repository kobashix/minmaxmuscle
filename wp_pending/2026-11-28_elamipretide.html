Elamipretide (SS-31): The FDA-Approved Mitochondrial Peptide — Research Dossier

Research compounds discussed for educational purposes. Not FDA-approved for human use where applicable. Consult a healthcare provider before use.

Most mitochondrial support products on the supplement shelf — CoQ10, NMN, PQQ — work around the edges of cellular energy production. Elamipretide (SS-31, brand name Forzinity) works at the core: it enters the mitochondria and physically stabilizes the inner membrane architecture where ATP is made. In September 2025, the FDA granted it accelerated approval, making it the first mitochondria-targeting peptide to reach regulatory approval in the United States. Here's what the science actually shows.

What Is Elamipretide?

Elamipretide is a synthetic tetrapeptide — four amino acids long — with the sequence D-Arg-Dmt-Lys-Phe-NH2, where Dmt is 2',6'-dimethyltyrosine, a modified tyrosine residue that contributes to the molecule's ability to scavenge reactive oxygen species. It was originally developed in the laboratory of Hazel Shen and Richard Schiller at Cornell University, which is why it carries the name Szeto-Schiller 31 (SS-31) in the academic literature. It has also been called MTP-131 and Bendavia in earlier clinical development stages.

The commercial version — Forzinity (40 mg/mL subcutaneous injection) — is manufactured by Stealth BioTherapeutics. The FDA granted it accelerated approval on September 19, 2025, specifically for improving muscle strength in patients with Barth syndrome who weigh at least 30 kg. Barth syndrome is a rare X-linked genetic condition caused by mutations in the TAFAZZIN gene that result in defective cardiolipin remodeling, impaired mitochondrial function, and severe heart and skeletal muscle weakness.

Mechanism of Action

To understand why elamipretide is different from existing mitochondrial supplements, it helps to understand what goes wrong in mitochondria during disease and aging.

The Cardiolipin Problem

Cardiolipin is a unique phospholipid found almost exclusively in the inner mitochondrial membrane. It serves as a structural scaffold that:

  • Maintains the folded architecture of the inner membrane (cristae)
  • Anchors and organizes the respiratory chain complexes (I, III, IV) into functional supercomplexes called the respirasome
  • Stabilizes ATP synthase (Complex V) in its active dimeric form
  • Recruits and tethers key mitochondrial proteins including cytochrome c

When cardiolipin is deficient (Barth syndrome), oxidized (ischemia, aging, heart failure), or its remodeling is disrupted, the cristae architecture collapses, the respiratory supercomplexes disassemble, electron leak increases, ROS production rises, and the efficiency of ATP synthesis falls. This is a final common pathway in multiple disease states.

How Elamipretide Works

Elamipretide's alternating aromatic-cationic motif allows it to penetrate cell membranes and concentrate in the inner mitochondrial membrane — crucially, without requiring a healthy transmembrane potential. This matters because diseased or aged mitochondria often lose their membrane potential, making potential-dependent mitochondria-targeting strategies ineffective. Once at the inner membrane, elamipretide:

  1. Binds cardiolipin directly, stabilizing cristae junctions and preventing their collapse.
  2. Maintains ETC supercomplex assembly — keeping respiratory complexes I, III, and IV organized in the respirasome, which channels electrons efficiently and minimizes electron leak.
  3. Scavenges reactive oxygen species via the Dmt residue, reducing oxidative damage to cardiolipin itself and to ETC components.
  4. Retains cytochrome c at the inner membrane, preventing its release into the cytoplasm (which triggers apoptosis).

The result is improved electron channeling, more efficient ATP production per oxygen consumed, and reduced mitochondrial ROS — a mechanistically distinct effect from CoQ10 (which shuttles electrons but does not anchor supercomplex architecture), NAD+ precursors (which raise metabolic substrate availability upstream), or antioxidants (which neutralize ROS downstream without addressing their source).

Key Research Findings

TAZPOWER Trial: FDA Approval Basis (Barth Syndrome)

The TAZPOWER study (NCT02616081) was a Phase 2/3 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial in 12 male patients aged 12–35 with genetically confirmed Barth syndrome. Participants received elamipretide 40 mg SC daily or placebo for 12 weeks, then crossed over after a washout period.

Endpoint Elamipretide Placebo Significance
Knee extensor peak work (primary) Significant improvement No significant change p < 0.05 (crossover comparison)
Open-label extension (168 weeks) Sustained strength benefit maintained — Long-term durability

The small sample size (n=12) reflects the rarity of Barth syndrome rather than scientific weakness — Barth syndrome affects approximately 1 in 300,000 to 400,000 males worldwide. The accelerated approval pathway was appropriate given the unmet need and the meaningful effect on the primary endpoint. Confirmatory trials are ongoing.

Heart Failure Studies

Elamipretide has been evaluated in heart failure across multiple studies. Heart failure — particularly the form characterized by preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), which is the dominant type in older adults — involves significant skeletal and cardiac muscle mitochondrial dysfunction, and cardiolipin oxidation has been identified as a contributing mechanism.

  • IDDEA-HF (Phase 2, HFrEF): A randomized, placebo-controlled trial of IV elamipretide in patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction showed no serious adverse events and favorable changes in left ventricular end-diastolic and end-systolic volumes at higher doses, though the NT-proBNP primary endpoint was not met. The trial established a human safety signal and suggested mechanistic activity.
  • Explanted failing hearts (mechanistic study): Ex vivo testing on myocardium from explanted failing human hearts showed that elamipretide significantly improved mitochondrial respiration efficiency — direct human tissue evidence for the drug's target mechanism.
  • 2026 Circulation: Heart Failure (HFpEF rat model): A 2026 study from the Heidelberg University group published in Circulation: Heart Failure tested elamipretide in a rat model of HFpEF and demonstrated significantly improved skeletal muscle performance — a clinically relevant finding given that exercise intolerance in HFpEF is driven in part by peripheral (skeletal muscle) rather than purely cardiac factors.

Aging and Sarcopenia: Preclinical Evidence

In aged mouse studies, elamipretide preserved cardiac and skeletal muscle function during the normal aging process without detectable changes in tissue epigenetic or transcriptomic aging markers. This finding — improved function without reverting the epigenetic age clock — suggests the drug works through a functional, bioenergetic mechanism rather than by broadly rejuvenating aging biology. The same research group noted that the effect was present in both male and female mice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is elamipretide (SS-31) FDA approved, and for what?

Yes. The FDA granted accelerated approval to elamipretide (brand name Forzinity) on September 19, 2025, for improving muscle strength in patients with Barth syndrome who weigh at least 30 kg. This makes it the first FDA-approved treatment for Barth syndrome and the first FDA-approved mitochondria-targeting peptide. The approval is based on knee extensor muscle strength improvements in the TAZPOWER trial (n=12 patients). The accelerated pathway means the approval rests on an intermediate clinical endpoint, and confirmatory trials are required. Forzinity is not approved for heart failure, aging, athletic performance, or any other indication.

How does elamipretide differ from CoQ10 or NAD+ supplements as a mitochondrial support compound?

Three key differences:

  1. Mechanism level: CoQ10 is an electron carrier (biochemical substrate), NAD+ precursors raise metabolic cofactor levels, but elamipretide operates at the structural level — stabilizing the protein-lipid architecture of the inner membrane itself.
  2. Supercomplex preservation: Only elamipretide has demonstrated the ability to maintain electron transport chain supercomplex (respirasome) assembly, which is critical for efficient electron channeling and low-leak ATP synthesis.
  3. Works in diseased mitochondria: Elamipretide penetrates mitochondria without requiring an intact transmembrane potential. CoQ10 and substrate-level approaches are less effective when mitochondria are already significantly impaired.

What other conditions is elamipretide being studied for beyond Barth syndrome?

Active research areas include:

  • Heart failure (HFrEF and HFpEF — ongoing preclinical and clinical investigation)
  • Age-related sarcopenia (preclinical data in aged mice)
  • Leber hereditary optic neuropathy (LHON) (early-phase studies in another mitochondrial disease)
  • Acute kidney injury (preclinical ischemia-reperfusion data)
  • Mitochondrial myopathies (Phase 2 MMPOWER study — did not meet primary functional endpoints)

No registered human longevity or healthy-aging trials exist as of November 2026. The aged-mouse data showing preserved muscle function without epigenetic aging changes makes elamipretide one of the more scientifically credible longevity-adjacent peptides in research — but this remains preclinical.

Research Limitations

  • Small trial size for FDA approval: The TAZPOWER pivotal trial enrolled 12 patients, reflecting disease rarity. The accelerated approval requires confirmatory data, and the full clinical benefit profile has not been established in larger populations.
  • Indication specificity: Current human evidence is primarily in Barth syndrome — a specific genetic condition with cardiolipin deficiency as its defining pathology. Extrapolating results to aging, acquired heart failure, or healthy adults involves significant uncertainty.
  • Mixed mitochondrial myopathy data: The Phase 2 MMPOWER trial in a broader population of mitochondrial myopathy patients did not meet its primary functional endpoints, suggesting the drug's effects may be strongest in conditions where cardiolipin dysfunction is the primary driver.
  • Heart failure primary endpoint not met: While human heart tissue data confirm the mechanistic target is engaged, the IDDEA-HF trial's NT-proBNP primary endpoint was not met, limiting the clinical claim for heart failure at present.
  • Short half-life: Elamipretide has a plasma half-life of approximately 4 hours after subcutaneous injection, requiring daily dosing — a contrast to weekly or monthly peptide competitors in metabolic disease.
  • Not commercially accessible for most research uses: Unlike many compounds in this database, Forzinity is a physician-prescribed FDA-approved drug, not a compound available from research chemical suppliers. Access outside clinical trials is limited to prescription use in Barth syndrome patients.
