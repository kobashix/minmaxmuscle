What Is BI 3034701?

BI 3034701 is an investigational peptide developed by Gubra A/S, a Danish specialty biopharma company, and licensed to Boehringer Ingelheim for global development and commercialization. It is a potential first-in-class triple receptor agonist simultaneously activating three receptors:

  • GLP-1R — glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor (target of semaglutide)
  • GIPR — glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide receptor (second target of tirzepatide)
  • NPY2R — neuropeptide Y type 2 receptor (novel central appetite target; not addressed by any approved obesity drug)

The NPY2R component is what distinguishes BI 3034701 from every other obesity drug in clinical development. While dozens of GLP-1R agonists and several GLP-1/GIP dual agonists have entered trials, no approved therapy or late-stage compound has combined these peripheral metabolic targets with a central NPY pathway suppressor. Boehringer Ingelheim initiated Phase 2 trials on July 16, 2026, triggering a EUR 10 million milestone payment to Gubra.

Mechanism of Action

GLP-1R and GIPR — The Established Foundation

GLP-1 receptor agonism is well-characterized: it stimulates insulin secretion, suppresses glucagon, delays gastric emptying, and induces satiety through hypothalamic and brainstem neuronal circuits. GIP receptor agonism at obesity-relevant doses enhances weight loss synergy with GLP-1R agonism through mechanisms that include central NPY neuron modulation and peripheral adipose tissue effects. These two targets are validated by tirzepatide's clinical success (20%+ weight loss in Phase 3).

NPY2R — The Novel Element

Neuropeptide Y (NPY) is one of the brain's most potent appetite stimulants. The hypothalamic arcuate nucleus releases NPY to drive food intake, particularly during caloric restriction or weight loss — this is a primary mechanism of compensatory hunger that makes long-term weight maintenance difficult.

NPY2R is a presynaptic inhibitory autoreceptor expressed on NPY-producing neurons. Activating NPY2R suppresses NPY release, thereby reducing central appetite drive at its source. The key hypothesis behind BI 3034701 is that by reducing compensatory NPY-driven hunger — which GLP-1 agonism does not directly address — the triple combination could sustain weight loss more durably and with less hunger rebound than dual GLP-1/GIP agonism alone.

Gubra had previously pioneered the GLP-1/Y2 co-agonist concept in research settings. BI 3034701 represents the full triple-receptor clinical application of this approach.

Key Research Findings

Phase 1 (NCT06352437) — Completed 2024–2025

BI 3034701 completed a Phase 1 first-in-human dose-escalation study. Boehringer Ingelheim reported "encouraging Phase 1 safety and tolerability data" as the basis for advancing to Phase 2. No detailed numerical data (dose levels, weight loss percentages, pharmacokinetics) have been published as of November 2026. The compound is administered as a subcutaneous injection.

Phase 2 (NCT07662122) — Initiated July 16, 2026

The Phase 2 trial is a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, dose-finding study in adults with overweight and obesity. It includes 7 arms:

  • Groups 1–6: Different doses of BI 3034701
  • Group 7: Placebo

All participants also receive dietary counseling and exercise guidance. Primary objectives include identifying optimal doses for Phase 3 and characterizing the efficacy and safety profile in a larger population. Completion timeline has not been publicly announced; Phase 2 results are not yet available.

Dosing in Research

BI 3034701 is administered as a subcutaneous injection. The specific Phase 1 dose levels and Phase 2 dose range have not been publicly disclosed. The dosing interval (weekly, biweekly, or monthly) has also not been confirmed in published materials. Given Gubra's expertise in long-acting peptide design, an extended-dosing interval is likely but not confirmed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes BI 3034701's NPY2 receptor targeting unique in obesity drug development?

BI 3034701 is the first obesity drug in clinical trials to combine GLP-1, GIP, and NPY2 receptor agonism in a single peptide. Neuropeptide Y (NPY) is the brain's most potent appetite stimulant — when dieting or weight loss reduces energy intake, the brain compensates by increasing NPY secretion to drive hunger. NPY2R is a presynaptic autoreceptor: activating it suppresses NPY release from hypothalamic neurons, directly reducing the central appetite drive that counteracts the satiety effects of GLP-1 agonism. No approved drug targets NPY2R. If the hypothesis holds, adding NPY2R agonism could reduce compensatory hunger — a key limitation of current GLP-1 therapies.

What data exists for BI 3034701, and what did Phase 1 show?

Boehringer Ingelheim reported "encouraging Phase 1 safety and tolerability data" after completing Phase 1 (NCT06352437), but no detailed numerical results have been publicly disclosed. Phase 2 (NCT07662122) initiated July 16, 2026. Phase 2 results are not yet available. The public evidence for the program rests on: (1) Gubra's published preclinical research on GLP-1/NPY2 co-agonism, (2) Phase 1 safety clearance, and (3) Boehringer Ingelheim's Phase 2 commitment and EUR 10M milestone to Gubra. This is an early-stage program — expect efficacy data no earlier than 2027–2028.

Who is Gubra and why does their role matter for BI 3034701?

Gubra (Copenhagen: GUBRA) is a Danish biopharma company founded in 2010, specializing in peptide-based drugs for metabolic diseases. Gubra's scientific heritage in GLP-1 and neuropeptide Y biology is the intellectual foundation for BI 3034701. The compound was licensed to Boehringer Ingelheim under a global agreement, with Gubra retaining milestone payments tied to development progress. The EUR 10M Phase 2 milestone confirms the program is advancing on schedule. Gubra's peptide design expertise — demonstrated through multiple discovery programs — provides credibility to the technical feasibility of the NPY2R-incorporating peptide format.

Research Limitations

  • No published efficacy data: Phase 1 numerical results have not been disclosed; Phase 2 is running. All efficacy claims are hypothetical or preclinical at this stage.
  • Novel mechanism with limited clinical validation: While NPY2R biology is well-established, NPY2R agonism as an anti-obesity strategy has not been clinically validated. BI 3034701 is testing this hypothesis for the first time in Phase 2.
  • Competitive market: The obesity drug space is crowded. Even if Phase 2 is positive, BI 3034701 faces competition from more advanced programs (tirzepatide, retatrutide) in Phase 3.
  • No long-term safety data: Phase 1 established preliminary tolerability; long-term safety data are unavailable.
  • Undisclosed dosing interval: Whether once-weekly, biweekly, or monthly dosing is feasible has not been confirmed publicly.

Bottom Line

BI 3034701 is a scientifically differentiated obesity peptide that adds NPY2 receptor agonism — central appetite suppression at the source — to the established GLP-1/GIP dual agonist framework. It is the first clinical-stage compound to test this triple mechanism. Phase 1 cleared safely; Phase 2 launched July 2026. No efficacy data are yet public. The key question is whether adding central NPY suppression produces meaningfully better weight loss or durability vs. dual GLP-1/GIP agonism — an answer that Phase 2 results (expected no earlier than 2027) will begin to address.
