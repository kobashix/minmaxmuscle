## Qualifying Peptides Added

### 1. Maridebart cafraglutide

- **Slug:** `maridebart-cafraglutide`
- **Category:** Metabolic & Weight Loss
- **Legal status:** Phase 3 Clinical
- **Primary focus:** Once-monthly GLP-1R agonist / GIPR antagonist antibody-peptide conjugate for obesity
- **Developer:** Amgen
- **Nicknames:** AMG 133, MariTide
- **Evidence quality:**
  - Phase 1 mechanistic data published in Nature Metabolism (PMC10896721): GIPR antagonism + GLP-1 agonism combination showed superior weight loss vs. either mechanism alone in preclinical + Phase 1 first-in-human data
  - Phase 2 trial (Jastreboff et al., NEJM, 2025): 592 adults, 11 dose groups
    - Obesity without T2DM: ~16.2% weight loss at 52 weeks (treatment-policy); ~16–20% (efficacy estimand) — weight still declining at 52 weeks
    - Obesity + T2DM: ~12.3% weight loss; HbA1c –1.6 pp
  - Phase 3 MARITIME program enrolling: MARITIME-1 (NCT06858839), MARITIME-HF (NCT07037459), MARITIME-CV (NCT07037433), MARITIME-3-J Japan (NCT06987695), vs. semaglutide in T2DM (NCT07866066)
- **Novelty check:** Not in existing site. Unique mechanism (GIPR antagonism vs. tirzepatide's GIP agonism) and unique dosing (once monthly — only such in pipeline). Qualifies.
- **Molecular note:** Technically an antibody-peptide conjugate (not a pure synthetic peptide). The GLP-1 agonist portion is a peptide; the GIPR antagonist portion is a full human antibody. Included with this note as it's widely tracked in peptide research communities and its pharmacological activity is peptide-driven. Rank set lower than pure peptide Phase 3 drugs to reflect this.
- **Reason added:** NEJM Phase 2 publication provides strong peer-reviewed evidence; once-monthly dosing is uniquely differentiating from all weekly agents; Phase 3 just launched makes this a timely discovery entry; GIPR antagonism mechanism is distinct from all existing site entries.

### 2. Cyclized Hexapeptide-9

- **Slug:** `cyclized-hexapeptide-9`
- **Category:** Skin & Hair Health
- **Legal status:** Cosmetic Grade
- **Primary focus:** Topical skin anti-aging peptide — outperforms retinol in published RCT for wrinkles, elasticity, pigmentation and barrier function
- **Nicknames:** CHP-9, Hexapeptide-9
- **Evidence quality:**
  - Published RCT: Chang et al., 2025, Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology (PMC12207714 / PubMed 40586182 / DOI: 10.1111/jocd.70290)
  - Design: 56-day randomized double-blind, three-arm (CHP-9 vs. retinol vs. vehicle), twice daily topical application
  - Outcomes: CHP-9 superior to retinol for wrinkle count/area (forehead + crow's feet), periorbital and nasolabial elasticity, UV pigmentation, barrier repair (TEWL)
  - Time-dependent potency augmentation: effect grew from Day 28 to Day 56, no plateau
  - Only tie: crow's feet roughness (equivalent to retinol)
  - Author affiliation with Shanghai Kans Biotech noted — sponsor-affiliated study
- **Novelty check:** Not in existing site. Previous run (2026-11-01) rejected it for "insufficient mechanistic and clinical data" — the published RCT in PMC12207714 constitutes the qualifying evidence that was previously absent. The trial was published June 2025 and indexed on PMC. Now qualifies.
- **Reason added:** First peer-reviewed RCT demonstrating superiority over retinol (the gold-standard cosmetic anti-aging ingredient) on nearly all endpoints; commercially available (can be recommended to readers now, unlike investigational drugs); fills a gap in site's Skin & Hair Health section which currently has no topical peptide entries.
